Who do we buy from, and what do our operations emit?

At IHS Towers I gathered procurement data for ESG reporting: local and foreign suppliers by count and spend, linked to emissions from diesel generators, offices and warehouses. This app rebuilds that report on public Canadian data.

What I built

  • Split suppliers into local and foreign by count and spend, using the cleaned supplier keys from my spend app.
  • Mapped local spend to provinces from the postal code.
  • Built a scope 1 and 2 view of federal facilities and fleet from the Greening Government open data.
  • Added a diesel generator calculator on the official ECCC factor.

From my IHS work, and what is new

  • From IHS: the report design, the local and foreign split, and the link to diesel, office and warehouse emissions.
  • New: every number here, all from public Government of Canada files.

Forecast to 2030

  • I tested ETS, AutoARIMA and a linear trend on held out years, with a seasonal naive line as the baseline. The screen shows the model with the lowest error for each series.
  • Federal operations: the linear trend had the lowest error, 4.60% against 5.34% for the baseline. It reaches 1,051.4 kt in FY'31, 66.1% above an SBTi 1.5C pathway of 633.1 kt from FY'20.
  • On yearly data, simple models are hard to beat. For federal operations, ETS and AutoARIMA both forecast a flat line at the latest value.
  • Canada and the US are shown too. The SBTi pathway is a company method, so for countries it is only an illustration.

What the numbers show

Most suppliers and most spend are local, and three foreign suppliers hold 73% of foreign spend. Federal scope 1 and 2 emissions are 42.5% below FY'06, and natural gas is the largest source. The two datasets sit side by side; supply chain emissions are not covered.

Data: Government of Canada Proactive Publication, Contracts and Greenhouse Gas Emissions Inventory, under the Open Government Licence, Canada. Diesel factor: ECCC Emission Factors and Reference Values, Version 4.0. Forecast: also Our World in Data (CC BY 4.0) and US EIA (public domain).
