Can a simple model give an honest heart risk estimate from public data?

I started with a Framingham dataset from a class assignment, then rebuilt the idea on US national survey data with real death records, and added a Canadian view.

What I built

  • Joined five NHANES survey cycles to the CDC death records and kept adults with no heart disease or stroke at the exam.
  • Trained a logistic regression and exported it to a small JSON file, so the calculator runs in the browser with no server.
  • Put the Framingham model next to it, with calibration charts for both.
  • Added weighted Canadian figures by province, age, sex, smoking and BMI.

From my assignment, and what is new

  • From the assignment: the choice of Framingham data, the BioLINCC reference, and the pandas clean up work.
  • New: every model, metric and chart, the NHANES and CCHS work, the JSON export, the app and the model card.

What the numbers show

The NHANES model ranks people well on unseen data (AUC 0.776), and age, smoking and blood pressure medicine move the estimate most. It rests on 239 deaths and on public records that NCHS partly altered for privacy, so it is a learning tool, not a clinical one. Framingham predicts a broader outcome, so its numbers run higher.

Data: CDC NHANES and NCHS linked mortality files, Framingham on Kaggle (license unknown, learning only), Statistics Canada CCHS 2022 under the Statistics Canada Open Licence.
