SCF 1. Fundamentals EOQ · Mississauga DC

Inventory drivers, replenishment math, and turns — vocabulary hiring managers expect before network or cash talk.

EOQ + orders per year (Mississauga CPG / retail SKU)

Retail · CPG · DC replenishment Mississauga DC

Statement: Annual demand 24,000 cases. Order / setup cost $180. Unit cost $28; holding rate 16%/yr → H = $4.48/case/yr (≈ $4.50). What EOQ minimizes order + holding cost, and how many orders per year?

Approach: EOQ = √(2DS/H). Orders/year = D/Q. Avg cycle stock = Q/2.

Module essay (skim optional)

Foundations of how goods, information, and cash move: roles (supplier → plant → DC → customer), inventory drivers (cycle stock, safety stock, pipeline), replenishment math (EOQ, reorder point, CSL), ABC prioritization, and turns / days-of-supply. Same patterns transfer across CPG, industrial, retail, and logistics.

SCD 2. Design Aggregate plan · Oakville

Where capacity and inventory absorb seasonality — structural choices that lock in cost-to-serve on Great Lakes corridors.

Aggregate plan for an Oakville plant (level vs chase vs hybrid)

Manufacturing · CPG · Industrial Seasonal NA demand

Statement: A GTA plant faces seasonal demand over 6 months. Compare level, chase, and hybrid plans. Which minimizes production + inventory + workforce cost?

Approach: Monthly balance sheets: production, ending inventory, hires/fires or overtime.

SCY 3. Dynamics Bullwhip · GTA corridor

Why a small Toronto retail wobble can look like a Michigan plant crisis — and how visibility dampens it.

Bullwhip on a Canada–US CPG corridor

CPG · Retail · Manufacturing Store → Mississauga DC → Detroit plant

Statement: End-customer demand at GTA stores is relatively smooth. With 2-week ordering cycles, safety buffers, and delayed POS visibility, how much do DC and plant orders amplify over 16 weeks?

Approach: Simulate chained reorder points; compare CV of demand vs orders at each tier.

Module essay (skim optional)

Demand amplification (bullwhip), lead-time and batching effects, information delay, and resilience under disruption. Same corridor dynamics show up in auto parts and grocery when status latency rises at the border.

Capacity-constrained product mix (Mississauga plant)

Manufacturing · Industrial · CPG Two SKUs · shared capacity

OTIF control tower for Great Lakes lanes

Logistics · Retail · 3PL Visibility · KPI design

Working capital — GTA distributor snapshot

Inventory · AR · AP Oakville / Mississauga ops

Statement: Annual COGS $48M, sales $72M. Average inventory $6.4M, AR $7.8M, AP $5.1M. What are DIO, DSO, DPO, and CCC? If we cut DIO by 8 days without hurting DSO/DPO, how much cash is released?

Approach: DIO = Inv / (COGS/365); DSO = AR / (Sales/365); DPO = AP / (COGS/365); CCC = DIO+DSO−DPO. Cash release ≈ ΔDIO × (COGS/365).

How SC decisions move cash

1
Inventory policy — EOQ / SS / newsvendor set DIO; level aggregate plans build stock ahead of peaks.
2
Payment terms — Longer supplier DPO frees cash; longer customer DSO consumes it.
3
Expediting & penalties — Rush freight and OTIF penalties are P&L hits lean inventory can trigger.
4
Investment lens — Inventory reduction projects compete with WMS / automation ROI on the same cash-flow frame.
